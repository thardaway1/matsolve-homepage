five areas

01

Energy & functional materials

Catalysts for energy conversion and optoelectronic materials, treated with the same tool: first-principles calculation of the site that decides the behaviour. CO2 adsorption on LSCM, CeO2 crystallography, H+ evolution under an interface dipole — and, on the optoelectronic side, a six-step design pipeline that runs data mining through to thermodynamic stability.

DFTCO2 reductionwater splittingβ-Ga2O3doping
02

Kinetics in solids and devices

Charge dynamics in quantum-dot devices and ionic diffusion in amorphous solids — two problems where the rate, not the ground state, is what matters. Atomistic impact-ionization results are carried into device simulation; amorphous structures are re-cast as graphs so a neural network can predict how diffusion changes as the structure does.

CQD IRPDimpact ionizationgraph neural networkLi diffusion
03

Materials data & ML-driven design

Experiments give roughly a hundred data points; simulation gives two hundred thousand. Hybridising the two is the lab's signature move — demonstrated on ceramic tape casting, then extended to novel-structure discovery, single-atom catalysts, and multimodal models that read SEM images and material tables together.

hybrid dataMEGNet · CGCNNSevenNetmachine visionSOFC
04

Geopolymer and composite

Geopolymers are still mixed by trial. Design of experiments plus metamodel-guided optimisation turns the mix ratio into a solvable multi-objective problem, and the resulting alkali-based matrix carries through to all-ceramic composites — hand lay-up, autoclave, then tensile, shear and compressive testing.

design of experimentskrigingPIAnOall-ceramic CMCTGA
05

Modeling of fabrication processes

Wide-bandgap semiconductor deposition reactors — HVPE and MOCVD — modelled for concentration, velocity and heating fields, so uniformity is a design variable rather than a discovered outcome. The same fluid-and-heat machinery is turned on engineering systems: component temperature, case flow, time-dependent behaviour, variable optimisation.

threads, traced through the papers

The five areas above are how the lab is organised. These are the arguments the publication record actually makes.

dielectrics
Predicted before they are made. BaTiO3-based dielectrics are governed by composition and processing at once, and the two are usually studied apart. Multi-modal models take both as input and stay interpretable — the model has to say which descriptor moved the permittivity, not just how much.J. Materiomics 2026 · Materials & Design 2026 · Mater. Today Commun. 2026 · J. Asian Ceram. Soc. 2025
defects
Wide-bandgap oxides. Doped β-Ga2O3, ZnO sheets and rutile TiO2 all fail or succeed at the same place: a defect site whose electronic structure resists direct measurement. First-principles calculations resolve those sites and connect them to opto-electronic and sensing behaviour.Physica B 2025 · RSC Advances 2025 · Surfaces and Interfaces 2026
tribo
Why triboelectricity is irreproducible. Contact electrification is treated as a material constant, yet measured values scatter across laboratories. Modelling homolytic bond rupture at the sliding interface shows the scatter is intrinsic — it comes from the mechanochemistry, not from sloppy experiments.Phys. Rev. Lett. 2023 · Small Methods 2026 · Nanoenergy Adv. 2025 · Adv. Electron. Mater. 2023
catalysis
Surfaces under operating conditions. Atomic-scale simulation of catalysis usually assumes a fixed Fermi level and a pristine surface. Neither holds in a running cell. The lab works on models that let the electronic boundary condition move with the experiment.J. Mater. Chem. A 2024 · Chem. Eng. J. 2025 · Mater. Today Energy 2026 · Nano-Micro Lett. 2026
composites
Curing, crystallisation, damage. Geopolymers and oxide/oxide ceramic matrix composites are still designed largely by trial. Metamodel-guided optimisation and molecular dynamics give the search a direction — including for composites that must survive very low Earth orbit.Digital Discovery 2025 · Adv. Compos. Hybrid Mater. 2026 · Composites Research 2025 · Adv. Compos. Mater. 2026

People

Who runs the solvers

A computational group of a dozen, split between physics-driven modelling and data-driven design.

principal investigator

Hyunseok Ko 고현석

Principal Investigator · Senior Researcher

Builds atomistic and data-driven models that explain why functional ceramics behave the way they do — and uses them to decide what to make next.

On the physics side, density functional theory resolves mechanisms that experiment alone cannot separate: vacancy-driven surface reactions, charge transfer at contact interfaces, dopant states in wide-bandgap oxides. On the data side, models map composition and processing onto measured properties, so a formulation decision can be made before the furnace is switched on.

The two halves are meant to be used together. Simulation datasets are large but idealised; experimental datasets are small but real. Much of the lab's recent work fuses them — hybridising a handful of measured samples with thousands of computed ones, and keeping the result interpretable enough that a materials scientist can argue with it.

Currently leading computational work on dielectric ceramics, energy-conversion surfaces, and inorganic composites. Organising committee, 9th Asian Materials Data Symposium (2026).
